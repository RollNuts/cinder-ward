# Audio Direction

## Benchmark Finding

Strong games do not rely only on "big music." They make actions feel credible with readable sound effects and leave room in the mix.

Observed clusters:

- Hades / Dead Cells: forward combat pulse, dry impact, aggressive transient sounds.
- Hollow Knight / Death's Door: melodic melancholy, spacious reverb, clear boss/intensity shifts.
- Darkwood / SIGNALIS: noise, drone, silence, unclear source position, oppressive room tone.
- Blasphemous / No Rest / V Rising: low ritual weight, bells, choir, heavy percussion, dark harmonic movement.
- Unrailed! / Overcooked: bright loop energy and highly readable interaction sounds.

## Initial 30-Second Cue

0-5s:

- No beat.
- Stone room air.
- Ember.
- Distant bell.
- Low D drone.

5-12s:

- Sparse 70-80 BPM pulse.
- Four-note plucked motif.
- Low strings or synthetic bowed texture.

12-21s:

- Combat layer enters only when danger crosses light.
- Dry hit.
- Short metallic scrape.
- Low drum.

21-27s:

- Music thins.
- Breath, cloth, fire, enemy movement.

27-30s:

- Ward flare or heavy strike hit.
- Bell plus low impact tail.

## Production Rule

Do not write "make it like Hades" or "make it like Hollow Knight" in generation prompts. Describe function, tempo, instrument family, space, and mix role.

Example:

> dark stone chapel, 72 BPM sparse plucked four-note motif, low bowed drone, distant bell, dry metallic hit layer, leave 2-6kHz space for attack effects

## Free/Low-Cost Tooling

- Audacity for editing and cleanup.
- Surge XT for synth/drone layers.
- GarageBand or LMMS for MIDI sketching.
- CC0/self-recorded samples preferred.

Avoid samples with unclear commercial rights. Credit-required material must be tracked before use.
